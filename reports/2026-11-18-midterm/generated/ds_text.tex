\newcommand{\DSResultsText}{%
With the maintained knowledge, the development agent passed \DSMaintainedPass{} of the applicable hidden checks, against \DSNonePass{} without knowledge and \DSSeedPass{} with the seed knowledge. Comparing per-run pass rates with a permutation test that shuffles condition labels within each task, maintained knowledge improved on no knowledge by \DSDiffMaintainedVsNone{} percentage points ($p=\DSPMaintainedVsNone$) and on the seed knowledge by \DSDiffMaintainedVsSeed{} points ($p=\DSPMaintainedVsSeed$). The seed knowledge made no measurable difference (\DSDiffSeedVsNone{} points, $p=\DSPSeedVsNone$). The judge also rated completion slightly higher with maintained knowledge (2.50 against 2.33 out of 3).

The improvement cost little. A task cost \DSNoneCost{} on average without knowledge, \DSSeedCost{} with seed knowledge, and \DSMaintainedCost{} with maintained knowledge, about 4 cents or 19\% more per task, mostly for reading 3 to 4 knowledge files. Median wall-clock time rose from 44~s to 47~s. The mean time is dominated by the long Actual Budget knowledge task, which took 356~s with maintained knowledge against 228~s without. Every run committed its work and none timed out. Only two of 120 runs (one seed, one maintained (A)) needed the fixed clarification reply.

In the post hoc sensitivity analysis (Amendment~2 of the protocol), the three ambiguous \code{maybe-knowledge} checks are dropped. The pass rates then become \DSSensNonePass{}, \DSSensSeedPass{}, and \DSSensMaintainedPass{}. The maintained-versus-none difference grows to \DSSensDiffMaintainedVsNone{} points ($p=\DSSensPMaintainedVsNone$).}

\newcommand{\DSDiscussionText}{%
\paragraph{Knowledge from strategy A versus strategy B.} The fourth condition gives the agent the knowledge written by the v2 B-Sonnet maintenance run instead of A-Sonnet's. It was added after the first three conditions had been analysed (Amendment~3), with everything else unchanged. With B's knowledge the agent passed \DSMaintainedBPass{} of the checks: \DSDiffMaintainedbVsNone{} points over no knowledge ($p=\DSPMaintainedbVsNone$), and \DSDiffMaintainedVsMaintainedb{} points below A's knowledge ($p=\DSPMaintainedVsMaintainedb$). Both patches recorded the \code{undici} decision, and both conditions passed those two checks in every run. The difference came mostly from Maybe. There, A's patch left 11 more entries than B's (25 against 17 application rows in core, 9 against 6 component entries), including how the activity components and their data object fit together. Neither patch states the design-token or pluralization conventions. Still, those checks rose to between 60\% and 100\% with A's knowledge and stayed near the no-knowledge level with B's (0\% to 60\%). A plausible explanation is that A's fuller description of the existing components led the agent to read and imitate them, but we cannot attribute the gain to a single entry. In this setting, the maintenance difference measured in v2 (17 against 10 of 21 required facts) carried over to development.

\paragraph{Where the effect comes from.} The clearest case is the Actual Budget webhook task. With maintained knowledge, all five runs imported \code{fetch} from \code{undici} and mocked \code{undici} in their tests, as the team had decided in an earlier session. Without knowledge, and with the seed knowledge, all ten runs used Node's global \code{fetch} and stubbed \code{globalThis.fetch}. This happened even though other modules in the same server import \code{undici}. Two checks went from 0\% to 100\% on this task alone (Table~\ref{tab:dstask}). Smaller gains came from functional design tokens in Maybe and from SSRF-safe fetching in Linkwarden. Checks whose answer was both in the maintained knowledge and visible in the code rose from 60\% to 77\%, and checks the code alone answers stayed at 89\% or higher in every condition (Table~\ref{tab:dscat}).

\paragraph{Why the seed knowledge did not help.} The seed knowledge was written before the v2 development, and part of it was stale. For Maybe it still described the old flat \code{*Component} naming that the code had abandoned. The maintenance run corrected exactly these entries. Agents with seed knowledge read the knowledge as often as agents with maintained knowledge (about four tool calls per run), so the difference lies in what they read, not whether they read it.

\paragraph{What did not improve.} The effect concentrated on the routine tasks (82\% to 97\%), not on the tasks we had labelled as knowledge tasks (74\% to 76\%). Two problems with our checks explain much of this. The Maybe badge checks assumed a design-system component, while the knowledge itself places domain components under \code{UI::}, and every run followed the knowledge. On Actual Budget, the check that the new half-year interval stays out of three other reports fell from 60\% to 0\% with maintained knowledge: those runs left the other reports untouched and said so in the commit message, but the judge sees only the diff and could not confirm it. In the same task, maintained-knowledge runs took longer and one committed a stray test configuration.

\paragraph{Cost of the knowledge.} The maintenance run that produced the patch cost \$1.40 on average per application push (A, Sonnet). With an average of 6.3 applicable checks per task, the 8-point gain is about half a review issue avoided per task. If the knowledge from one push is reused in ten development tasks, maintenance adds \$0.14 per task on top of the \$0.04 extra development cost.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Six tasks, five repetitions, one development model, and one model judge that sees only the diff. The knowledge was learned from simulated developers, and the hidden checks were written by the same team that designed the tasks. Two checklist problems were found after the runs; we report them instead of rewriting the checks. These results support a measurable benefit of maintained knowledge in this setting, not a general claim.}

\newcommand{\DSConclusionText}{With maintained knowledge a development agent passed 86\% of hidden review checks against 78\% without knowledge ($p=0.02$, five runs per task and condition), at about 4 cents more per task. Stale seed knowledge gave no benefit, so the value comes from keeping knowledge current, not from having a knowledge file. Knowledge maintained by strategy B helped less than A's (81\%, 6 points below A, $p=0.013$), so the coverage difference between the strategies carries over to development.}
