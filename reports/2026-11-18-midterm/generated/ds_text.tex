Pooled results were 146/188 (77.7\%) with no knowledge, 146/190 (76.8\%) with seed knowledge, 161/188 (85.6\%) with maintained A, and 152/188 (80.9\%) with maintained B. A minus B is +4.8 pooled points, while the original task-weighted contrast was +6.1 points ($p=0.0129$). These tests retain their original weighting; the pooled percentages do not. In this comparison, maintained A knowledge improved observed checks; stale seed knowledge did not demonstrate a benefit.

The mean development cost was \DSNoneCost{} without knowledge, \DSSeedCost{} with seed knowledge, and \DSMaintainedCost{} with maintained A, about 19\% more for A than none. Median time rose from 44 to 47 seconds; mean time rose from 75 to 96 seconds. The long Actual Budget knowledge task influenced the mean, taking 228 seconds without knowledge and 356 seconds with A. Client-side list-price estimates are reported, not subscription invoices. Development cost excludes maintenance cost. In the original post hoc sensitivity analysis (Amendment 2), three ambiguous \code{maybe-knowledge} checks were excluded: \code{ds-base}, \code{ds-namespace}, and \code{preview-path}. \newcommand{\DSDiscussionText}{%
\paragraph{Knowledge from strategy A versus strategy B} The fourth condition used knowledge written by the v2 B-Sonnet maintenance run instead of A-Sonnet's. It was added after the three original conditions had been analysed. Pooled B results were 152/188 (80.9\%), 3.2 points above none; the original task-weighted B-none contrast was +2.1 points ($p=0.5774$). The pooled A-B difference was 4.8 points; its original task-weighted contrast was +6.1 points ($p=0.0129$). The task-weighted estimates differ from the pooled rates. Both patches recorded the \code{undici} decision, and both conditions passed those two checks in every run. The A patch contained more Maybe entries than B, including descriptions of activity components and their data object. A plausible explanation is that A's fuller description led the agent to read and imitate existing components, but these runs do not attribute the gain to a particular entry.

\paragraph{Where outcomes varied} Routine tasks improved from 82\% to 97\% between none and A, while the tasks labeled knowledge tasks moved from 74\% to 76\%. This does not support the claim that benefits occur only when code is silent. A clear task-level observation is the Actual Budget webhook task: all five A runs imported \code{fetch} from \code{undici} and mocked it, while all ten none and seed runs used Node's global \code{fetch}. Other task outcomes and checklist limitations are described in Tables~\ref{tab:dstask} and~\ref{tab:dscat}. In Maybe, badge checks assumed a design-system component while the knowledge placed domain components under \code{UI::}. In Actual Budget, the check that the new half-year interval stays out of three other reports fell from 60\% to 0\% with maintained knowledge: those runs left the reports untouched and said so in the commit message, but the judge could not confirm this from the diff. Maintained-knowledge runs on that task took longer and one committed a stray test configuration. These issues temper task-level interpretation and do not establish a universal effect or mechanism.

\paragraph{Why the seed knowledge did not demonstrate a benefit} The seed knowledge was written before v2 development and included a stale Maybe component naming scheme that the code had abandoned. Maintenance corrected these entries. This comparison supports a benefit for the maintained A condition in these tasks; it does not establish that stale knowledge is always harmful or that any knowledge file is beneficial.

\paragraph{Cost} The development mean rose from \$0.202 to \$0.240 per task. The v2 A-Sonnet maintenance that produced the knowledge used in this study cost \$1.40 on average per application push, separately from development. If that knowledge were reused for ten development tasks, allocating its maintenance cost equally would add \$0.14 per task. This reuse example is an assumption, not a measured amortized cost. All costs are client-side list-price estimates, not subscription invoices.

\paragraph{Limitations} Six tasks, five repetitions, one development model, and one model judge that sees only the diff. The knowledge was learned from simulated developers, and the hidden checks were written by the same team that designed the tasks. Two checklist problems were identified after the runs and are reported without rewriting the checks. The results support selected observations in this setting, not a general claim.}
